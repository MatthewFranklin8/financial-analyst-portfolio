FP&A Modeling · Variance Analysis

Netflix FP&A Model & Variance Analysis

How should a forecast be revised once actual results come in, and what actually drove the difference?

A driver-based revenue forecast and scenario model built from Netflix's SEC filings (10-K, 10-Q, 8-K shareholder letters), with a full Q2'26 forecast-vs-actual variance bridge across four regions.

Q2'26ForecastActualVariance
Total Revenue$12,707M$12,560M($147M) −1.2%
Operating Margin30.0%33.4%+3.4pp
Operating Income$3,812M$4,193M+$381M +10.0%
Finding: the $381M operating income beat came entirely from margin, not revenue. The revenue miss cost about $44M in profit, while margin outperformance added $425M. Netflix attributes the margin strength to favorable expense timing, so the model does not carry it forward into future quarters.

Recommendation: hold Q3'26–Q1'27 margin assumptions near trailing average, revisit UCAN/APAC growth assumptions given a multi-quarter slowdown, and hold or raise the LATAM growth assumption after two straight quarters of outperformance.

DCF Valuation · Relative Valuation

ExxonMobil DCF Valuation

Is ExxonMobil stock worth what it is currently trading at?

A five-year discounted cash flow model (8.20% WACC, 2.50% terminal growth) cross-checked against a relative valuation versus Chevron and Shell.

CompanyP/EDividend Yield
ExxonMobil (XOM)~20.6x~2.6%
Chevron (CVX)~18.9x~3.6%
Shell (SHEL)~12.4x~3.5%
Finding: the DCF estimates fair value at roughly $132/share, below the trading price, and the relative valuation points the same direction; ExxonMobil trades at the highest multiple and lowest yield of the three. Sensitivity to WACC and terminal growth is wide ($98–$201), so the conclusion is read as directional, not a precise target.

As of August 2026, the Strait of Hormuz shipping disruption from the Middle East conflict is the largest near-term factor for ExxonMobil's results, larger than any single modeling assumption.
